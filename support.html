Where is the app?

Timezoner lives in the menu bar and has no Dock icon and no main window. After you open it, look at the right-hand side of the menu bar for your clocks. Click them to open the panel; everything else is one click away from there.

If you launch it again later, nothing appears to happen — that is expected. It is already running, in the menu bar.

Common questions

Adding and arranging timezones

Click the menu bar item → Settings… → Timezones → Add Timezone. Search by city, airport code (SFO, NYC) or "GMT". Search runs entirely offline.

Drag the handle on the left of a row to reorder. That order is the order everywhere — menu bar and panel.

Renaming a zone

Click its name in the Timezones list and type. "Mom", "Dublin office" — whatever is useful to you. Clear the field to go back to the city name.

The menu bar shows "TOK" but I want something else

That is the Short name column, next to the name. It starts out derived from the city, and you can type anything you like over it. Clearing it restores the default.

The short name is used in the compact menu bar layout. In Normal layout the full name is shown instead, so both are worth setting if you switch between them.

Turning off the weekday

In Normal layout a zone on a different date from home is tagged with its weekday, like Tokyo 03:37 Thu. General → Show the weekday switches that off, and narrows the menu bar item at the same time.

Showing seconds

Per zone, in the Timezones list. Note that a zone with seconds enabled updates every second rather than every minute, which costs a little more energy.

Time travel

The slider in the panel moves every clock together, so you can see what time a meeting lands in each place. Arrow keys step it; hold ⌥ for 5-minute steps and ⇧ for 1-hour steps. Click NOW, or press Esc, to return to the present.

Alarms

Settings → Alarms. Alarms are delivered by macOS notifications, so Timezoner asks for notification permission the first time you create one.

Troubleshooting

My clocks disappeared from the menu bar

macOS hides menu bar items when the bar runs out of room — apps with many icons, or a laptop with a notch, make this common. Timezoner shrinks itself automatically as space gets tight, and will fall back to a small clock icon rather than vanish, but the system can still push it out entirely.

Try: quitting a few other menu bar apps, or switching to Compact layout in General, which uses roughly half the width per timezone.

Alarms are not firing

Check System Settings → Notifications → Timezoner and make sure notifications are allowed. If permission was refused, Timezoner cannot ask again — the Open Notification Settings… item in its menu takes you straight there.

Alarms cannot wake a sleeping Mac. macOS only allows that for system-level alarms, so a Timezoner alarm that comes due while the Mac is asleep is delivered when it wakes.

Times look wrong after a DST change

Timezoner reads the timezone database built into macOS, so it follows daylight-saving rules automatically. If a time still looks wrong, check that your Home timezone in General is the one you expect — every day marker and every "+5h" difference is measured against it.

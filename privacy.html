Privacy Policy

Timezoner for macOS

Timezoner collects nothing, transmits nothing, and has no way to do either. It contains no networking code at all.

That is not a promise about how we handle your data. It is a statement about what the app can do: there is no code in Timezoner that opens a network connection, so there is no data to collect, no server to send it to, and no third party to share it with.

Data collected

None. Timezoner has no accounts, no sign-in, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs or libraries of any kind. It uses only frameworks that ship with macOS.

Because nothing is collected, there is nothing to share, sell, or disclose to anyone — including us. We never see your data, because it never leaves your Mac.

Data stored on your Mac

Timezoner saves your settings locally so it can restore them the next time it launches. That includes:

  • The timezones you have added, and the order you put them in
  • Any custom names or menu bar short names you type for them
  • Your alarms, including their labels and times
  • Preferences such as the menu bar layout, time format and home timezone

All of it is written to your app's own preferences on your own Mac, inside the sandbox container macOS gives the app. Nothing in that list is transmitted anywhere, and no part of Timezoner reads it back out for any purpose other than drawing your clocks.

Timezone data

City names, timezone rules and daylight-saving transitions all come from the timezone database that is already part of macOS. Timezoner performs no lookups over the network — it works on a plane, and searching for a city sends nothing anywhere.

Permissions

Notifications

Optional, and only requested if you create an alarm. Alarms are delivered by macOS on your Mac. The alarm's label — which you typed — is handed to the system notification service so it can be shown to you, exactly as with any other local notification. That means macOS stores it in its own notification history and can show it on the Lock Screen, according to the settings you choose for Timezoner in System Settings → Notifications. It stays on this Mac: Timezoner uses only local notification triggers, has no push capability, and sends nothing over the internet.

You can change or withdraw this permission at any time in System Settings → Notifications → Timezoner. If you refuse it, alarms will not fire; nothing else changes.

Open at Login

Optional, off by default, and toggled in Timezoner's settings. It registers the app with macOS's login-items service and involves no data.

Retention and deletion

Your settings stay on your Mac until you delete them. To remove everything Timezoner has stored, drag the app to the Trash and then delete its container:

~/Library/Containers/com.xiaoyanwu.timezoner

You can also remove individual timezones or alarms inside the app at any time. There is no copy anywhere else to request, correct, or erase, because no copy was ever made.

Children

Timezoner is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, the revised version will be published on this page with a new date below. Should a future version of Timezoner ever gain a feature that transmits anything, that will be described here before the feature ships.

Links to this site

Timezoner's About screen links to this page and to its support page. Following one opens your default web browser, which then makes its own request to whoever hosts this site — so that host sees your IP address and browser, as with any web page you visit. Timezoner itself makes no request; it only asks macOS to open the link.
